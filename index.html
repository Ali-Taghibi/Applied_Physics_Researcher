EXECUTIVE BIOGRAPHY & SCIENTIFIC TRAJECTORY

From Subatomic Physics to Industrial Scale Clean Technologies and Clinical Impact

My academic journey began with a B.Sc. in Physics at Damghan University (2011–2015), followed by graduate research in Applied Physics at K. N. Toosi University of Technology (2015–2018). Throughout my career, my core scientific mission has been to bridge high-precision Monte Carlo particle transport codes (Geant4-DNA, MCNP6, MCNPX, and FLUKA) with urgent environmental and medical engineering challenges.

In environmental technology, I model the nanoscale radiolysis of water and wastewater under high-energy electron beams (EBWT/EBFGT)—showing how reactive species (•OH, e⁻_aq, H•) mineralize persistent trace micropollutants that conventional treatments fail to remove. In nuclear medicine and radiation protection, I have co-designed heavy-water (D₂O) hybrid targets for accelerator-based Molybdenum-99 (⁹⁹Mo) production, authored NCRP 151 labyrinth shielding frameworks, and coordinated interdisciplinary R&D on smart fiber-optic dosimetric fabrics.

Bridging Subatomic Monte Carlo Physics with Macro-Scale Environmental & Medical Solutions.

My research bridges fundamental track-structure radiation physics with applied industrial engineering through high-precision Monte Carlo particle transport. Utilizing specialized computational toolkits including Geant4-DNA, MCNP6, MCNPX, and FLUKA, I investigate the nanoscale stochastic interactions of ionizing radiation with condensed matter, translating fundamental energy deposition and radical yield phenomena into scalable environmental and nuclear solutions.

This computational framework powers multi-scale investigations across three core domains: predictive kinetic modeling of electron-beam wastewater radiolysis (EBWT/EBFGT) to mineralize recalcitrant micropollutants, neutronic and thermal-mechanical optimization of heavy-water (D₂O) hybrid targets for non-HEU Molybdenum-99 (⁹⁹Mo) medical isotope production, and rigorous NCRP 151 labyrinth shielding design for high-power industrial accelerators alongside smart fiber-optic dosimetry.

2. Evolution of Electron-Beam Flue Gas Treatment (EBFGT) (1970s–Present)

Click on milestones to explore dry radiolytic oxidation, ammonium fertilizer byproduct recovery, and SO₂/NOₓ removal chemistry.

1970s DISCOVERY

Simultaneous SO₂/NOₓ Radiolytic Oxidation & Ammonia Injection

Pioneering batch experiments by Ebara Corp. and JAERI demonstrated that irradiating humid flue gas with electrons generates oxidizing radicals (•OH, O•, HO₂•) that convert SO₂ and NOₓ into aerosols, which neutralize with ammonia into dry solid fertilizer.

Key Technical Parameters:
• Beam Energy: 0.3 – 0.75 MeV
• Pathway: Humid N₂/O₂ Radiolysis
• Recovery: (NH₄)₂SO₄ & NH₄NO₃ Powder
